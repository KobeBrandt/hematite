Contour

A design system in plain CSS, shaped like the MX Master 4: a graphite or pale grey shell, sculpted curves, soft light from above, fine graphite grain, keys with a dished top, and knurled metal as the only shiny part.

01

Principles

The look comes from an object you hold all day: a sculpted mouse. Matte surfaces, curves that fit the hand, light that falls from above, metal only where something turns, and one light that tells you what is live. Every rule below still comes from established UI-design, CSS-architecture and accessibility practice.

CRAP: Contrast

Graphite on pale grey

Graphite text on a pale grey shell, 13:1, and the reverse in dark mode: sharp but never glaring. Every colour pair stays between 4.5:1 and 15:1, and control edges stay above 3:1 even though they look soft.

CRAP: Repetition

One material, three heights

Every surface is the same matte shell with a hairline and a top sheen. Three soft shadows say how high it sits. Radii grow with size and stay concentric. If it looks different, it behaves differently.

CRAP: Alignment

Left, on a grid

Text is left aligned and capped at 65 characters. Layout primitives snap to one container and gutter.

CRAP + Gestalt: Proximity

Space groups things

Micro space inside components, macro space between sections. Related items sit closer than unrelated ones.

Colour: 60-30-10

Shell, graphite, metal

About 60% pale shell, 30% graphite, 10% brushed metal. No brand colour: metal marks the one main action, graphite keys mark what is pressed, and a small green light marks what is current.

Efficiency

HTML first

System fonts, no images, native elements wherever they exist. Every component works without JavaScript; an optional 5 KB script adds tab keys, dialogs and counters.

Nielsen's heuristics, built in

HeuristicHow the system covers it
Visibility of system statusAlerts, notification banners, toasts, loaders and progress bars with live regions; step indicators and task lists; aria-current everywhere.
Match with the real worldTemplates and patterns in plain language: "Check your answers", "Booking confirmed", not "Submit form".
User control and freedomBack links in every flow, Change links on check answers pages, dialogs that close with Escape, cancel links next to actions.
Consistency and standardsAll values come from tokens. Links are always underlined; buttons are always keys.
Error preventionHints above the field, inputs sized to the answer, a confirm dialog before destructive actions, warning text for consequences.
Recognition rather than recallVisible labels on every input, combo box suggestions, summary lists that show answers back.
Aesthetic and minimalist designNo brand colour, one material, gradients only as light on a surface, metal only on parts that move.
Help users recover from errorsError summary that links to each field, errors that start with "Error" and say how to fix it, error pages that say what to do next.
